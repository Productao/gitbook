# Slide U2 — Use Case: Span of Control Analysis

## Title
Use Case: Span of Control Analysis

## Lead-line
Identify management compression and overload — then model structural improvements.

## Goal
Analyze how management is distributed across your organization and identify positions with unhealthy spans (too few or too many direct reports), then model corrections.

## Step-by-Step Workflow

### 1. Open the Analysis
- Go to **Main Org** → switch to **Workforce Hub** (keyboard shortcut: 4)
- Open the **Layers & Spans of Control** chart

### 2. Configure the Chart
- Set SOC groupings: 0 (ICs), 1–2 (compression), 3–4 (narrow), 5–9 (healthy), 10–15 (wide), 16–25 (overloaded), 26+ (extreme)
- Review the distribution matrix — look for concentrations in the 1–2 range (compression) and 16+ range (overload)

### 3. Identify Patterns
- **Compression red flag:** >30% of managers have SOC 1–2 — you're paying for management overhead without proportional value
- **Overload red flag:** Multiple managers with SOC 16+ — they're likely bottlenecks for their teams
- **Inconsistency flag:** Similar roles at similar levels with wildly different spans

### 4. Drill into Specifics
- Switch to **Org Chart** → use **Spotlight** to highlight positions with SOC < 3 or SOC > 15
- Click through flagged positions to understand the context — some narrow spans are legitimate (e.g., specialized technical leads)

### 5. Model Corrections
- Create a **scenario** → consolidate compressed managers (merge two teams under one manager)
- Reduce overloaded managers by splitting teams or adding a middle layer
- **Check Workforce Hub** Before/After to validate improvements

### 6. Present Findings
- Export the Layers & Spans chart (Before and After) as PowerPoint
- Export the scenario comparison showing structural improvements

## Key Metrics to Check
- SOC distribution by layer (before vs. after)
- Manager-to-IC ratio
- 1:1 Managers Ratio (what percentage of managers have only 1 report)
- Total number of management layers

## Speaker Notes
SOC analysis is one of the highest-ROI analyses you can do in Agentnoon. Every manager with 1–2 direct reports represents a potential efficiency opportunity — that's management cost for minimal management span. Conversely, managers with 20+ reports are often overwhelmed and can't give adequate attention to their teams. The data from Workforce Hub makes this conversation objective rather than political — you're presenting numbers, not opinions.
