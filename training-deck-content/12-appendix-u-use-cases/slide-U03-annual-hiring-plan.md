# Slide U3 — Use Case: Building an Annual Hiring Plan

## Title
Use Case: Building an Annual Hiring Plan

## Lead-line
Model phased headcount growth across departments — with budget validation and scenario comparison.

## Goal
Build a realistic, phased hiring plan for the upcoming fiscal year that shows when hires happen, what they cost, and how they affect the org structure over time.

## Step-by-Step Workflow

### 1. Gather Inputs
- Collect hiring requests from department heads: positions needed, target start quarters, priority (critical vs. nice-to-have)
- Get budget guidance from Finance: total headcount budget, cost targets, constraints

### 2. Create Scenario Variants
- Create **2–3 scenarios** representing different approaches:
  - **Conservative** — Critical hires only, phased slowly
  - **Target** — Expected plan, balanced phasing
  - **Aggressive** — All requested hires, front-loaded
- Name clearly: "FY26 Hiring Plan — Conservative," "FY26 Hiring Plan — Target," etc.

### 3. Add Positions with Hire Dates
- In each scenario, add the planned positions under the appropriate managers
- **Set hire dates** for each position — spread realistically across the year (not all on Jan 1)
- Use **Rate Cards** (if configured) to auto-populate salaries based on level and location
- Fill in department, location, pay grade, and other relevant fields

### 4. View in Forecast
- Switch to **Forecast** → set to **Quarterly** time period
- Toggle between **Headcount** and **Cost** to see both dimensions
- Group by **Department** to see where growth is concentrated
- Review **Show Changes** to see the net additions per quarter

### 5. Compare Scenarios
- Go to homepage → select two scenarios → **Compare**
- Review side-by-side: total cost, headcount by quarter, structural impact
- Present options to leadership with clear trade-offs

### 6. Submit for Approval
- Finalize the preferred scenario → add justification with budget alignment notes
- Submit through OpEx Panel
- Export Forecast data as CSV for Finance and recruiting

## Key Metrics to Check
- Quarterly headcount by department
- Quarterly cost by department
- Budget variance (planned cost vs. budget target)
- Hiring pace (are hires realistically spread or clustered?)

## Screenshot / Visual
Forecast view showing quarterly headcount stepping up across the year, grouped by department.

## Speaker Notes
The most common mistake in hiring plans is putting all hire dates on the first day of the quarter. In reality, hires happen throughout the quarter — spread dates across the months for a more realistic plan. Rate Cards save enormous time here: if you have compensation bands configured, you don't have to manually look up salaries for each new position. Creating three variants (Conservative, Target, Aggressive) gives leadership options and shows the trade-offs — "for $500K more, we get 8 additional engineers and accelerate the product roadmap by one quarter."
