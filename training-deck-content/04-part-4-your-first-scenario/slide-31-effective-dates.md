# Slide 31 — Setting Effective Dates

## Title
Setting Effective Dates

## Lead-line
Control when each change takes effect — this is how you build a phased plan instead of an all-at-once flip.

## Key Details
- **Three date types, three purposes:**
  - **Hire Date** — When a new position becomes active (use for planned hires). The position appears in Forecast starting from this date.
  - **Termination Date** — When a closed position is eliminated (use for RIFs/exits). The position disappears from Forecast on this date.
  - **Effective Date** — When a modification takes effect (use for moves, title changes, salary adjustments). The old state shows before this date; the new state shows after.
- **Why this matters:** Without effective dates, all changes appear as if they happen immediately. With them, you get a realistic timeline in Forecast — e.g., 5 hires in Q1, 3 in Q2, 2 in Q3.
- **Set dates when making changes:** When adding, closing, or editing a position, look for the date field in the edit panel. Always set it if the change has a specific timing.
- **Forecast integration:** Effective dates feed directly into Forecast. Switch to Forecast view to see your changes phased over time by month, quarter, or year.

## Speaker Notes
Effective dates are one of the most powerful features in Agentnoon, and they're also one of the most commonly missed. If you add 10 positions and don't set hire dates, they all show up as headcount immediately — which inflates your current numbers. Always set a hire date for new positions, a termination date for closures, and an effective date for modifications. This turns your scenario from a static "before and after" into a dynamic, time-phased plan. Your approvers and Finance partners will appreciate the precision.
