# Slide 40 — Commenting

## Title
Commenting

## Lead-line
Document decisions, gather feedback, and build an audit trail — all within the scenario.

## Key Details
- **Scenario-level comments:** Open the **Comments** panel from the side toolbar → type your comment → click Post. These are general comments visible to everyone with access to the scenario.
- **Position-level comments:** Click on a specific position card → add a comment directly on that position. These are targeted and help explain why a specific change was made.
- **Use cases:** Ask questions ("Should we backfill this role?"), document decisions ("Finance approved the $150K budget for this hire"), flag concerns ("This team's SOC will be 18 after this move — is that okay?"), and request review ("Ready for your input on the Sales restructure").
- **Resolve comments:** When a comment has been addressed, click Resolve. It stays in the record but is removed from the active list. Authors and scenario owners can delete comments if needed.

## Screenshot / Visual
The Comments panel open, showing a few example comments — including a question, a decision note, and a resolved comment.

## Speaker Notes
Comments are underused but incredibly valuable. They serve two purposes: collaboration during the planning process, and documentation afterward. When an approver asks "why did you eliminate this role?" three months later, the comments tell the story. A best practice is to comment liberally as you build — note your reasoning for major changes, flag anything you want a reviewer to look at, and document any decisions made in meetings. Think of comments as the "meeting notes" that live right inside the plan.
