# Slide 41 — Real-Time Collaboration

## Title
Real-Time Collaboration

## Lead-line
See who's working in the scenario with you and coordinate to avoid conflicts.

## Key Details
- **Presence indicators:** Profile icons appear at the top of the scenario showing who currently has it open. You can see at a glance who's working alongside you.
- **Coordination best practices:**
  - Announce which section or department you're working on (via comments or a quick message to your co-planner)
  - Work in different areas of the org to avoid overlapping changes
  - Limit concurrent editors to 2–3 people for the best experience
- **Review together:** Before submitting for approval, do a final review with your co-planners. Switch to "Show Changes" view and walk through the modifications together.
- **When to collaborate vs. work solo:** Simple plans (adding a few hires) are fine solo. Complex reorgs spanning multiple departments benefit from collaborative editing.

## Screenshot / Visual
The top of the scenario workspace showing presence indicators — 2–3 profile icons indicating multiple people working in the scenario simultaneously.

## Speaker Notes
Real-time collaboration works best with a bit of coordination. Unlike a Google Doc where two people can edit the same paragraph, org design works better when people stay in their lanes — literally. If you're restructuring Engineering and your colleague is restructuring Sales, you can both work in the same scenario without stepping on each other. The presence indicators help you know who's around. If you see someone's icon appear, send them a quick message to coordinate. And always do a joint review before submission — two sets of eyes catch things one set misses.
