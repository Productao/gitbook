# Slide 7 — Positions vs People

## Title
Positions vs People

## Lead-line
Agentnoon is position-based — you plan by creating and modifying positions first, then assigning people to them.

## Key Details
- **Position** = A job role in your organization, whether filled or open. Positions have attributes like title, department, salary range, and reporting structure. Positions persist even when no one is assigned to them.
- **People (Employee)** = The individual assigned to a position. One person fills one position at a time. People have attributes like name, email, start date, and employee ID.
- **Why this matters:** You can plan for roles that don't have anyone in them yet. Create a "Senior Engineer" position, set the salary and hire date, and assign someone later — the role exists in your plan either way.
- **In practice:** When you model a reorg, you're moving positions (roles) around the org chart. The people assigned to those positions come along for the ride.

## Speaker Notes
This is the single most important concept in Agentnoon. Unlike a headcount-based system where you're just tracking numbers, Agentnoon lets you plan at the position level. This means you can design your future org structure — every role, every reporting line, every salary — before you've hired a single person. Vacant positions show up in the org chart and in your headcount. This lets you build a hiring plan that's structurally complete, not just a number in a spreadsheet.
