# Slide A7 — Activity Analysis

## Title
Activity Analysis

## Lead-line
Map business activities across your org and see where effort is being spent — value-add vs. non-value-add.

## What Is It?
Activity Analysis lets you define business processes (e.g., Sales, Finance, Product Development) and allocate each position's time across those activities as a percentage. This reveals how effort is distributed and whether resources are aligned to strategic priorities.

## How to Set It Up
1. Open a **scenario** and click the **Activity Analysis** icon
2. Add **Level 1 business processes** at the root (e.g., Sales, Finance, Product, Operations)
3. Add **Level 2 activities** under each process (e.g., under Sales: Offline Sales, Conferences, Account Management)
4. Categorize each activity as **Value Add** (directly contributes to business goals) or **Non-Value Add** (necessary overhead but not directly productive)

## How to Use It
1. **Assign positions to activities** with time allocation percentages (e.g., a Sales Manager spends 60% on Account Management, 20% on Conferences, 20% on Reporting)
2. Navigate the **activity tree** in the left panel to expand/collapse processes
3. Switch to **Workforce Hub** → use the **Activity Split by Category** chart to visualize effort distribution
4. Analyze by **FTE Count** or **Cost** — see where the most expensive effort is going
5. **Filter by activity level** to drill into specific processes
6. View as **Absolute numbers, Total %, or Group %** for different perspectives

## Speaker Notes
Activity Analysis is an advanced feature used primarily for organizational efficiency reviews. It answers questions like: "How much of our engineering team's time is spent on value-add product development vs. non-value-add reporting?" and "Are we investing enough resources in our strategic growth areas?" It requires some setup — you need to define your activity taxonomy and allocate percentages — but the insights are powerful for resource optimization conversations.
