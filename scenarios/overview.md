# Scenarios Overview

Scenarios are Agentnoon's powerful what-if planning tool. They let you model organizational changes in a safe sandbox environment, see the exact cost and headcount impact, and compare different approaches before implementing anything in real life.

## What Are Scenarios?

A **scenario** is an editable copy of your org chart where you can freely make changes without affecting your live organization. Think of it as a playground for testing ideas.

**In a scenario, you can:**
- Add new positions
- Close existing positions (RIF - Reduction in Force)
- Move people and teams to different managers
- Edit position details (title, salary, department, etc.)
- Reorganize entire departments
- Model future org structures

**Key principle:** Changes in scenarios are **completely isolated** from your Main Org. Nothing you do in a scenario affects your live data until you choose to implement it.

## Why Use Scenarios?

Scenarios answer critical questions that spreadsheets and mental models can't:

**"What if we reorganized the engineering team?"**
- See the visual org chart with the new structure
- Understand exactly who reports to whom
- Identify any gaps or issues before committing

**"How much would it cost to add 15 positions in Q2?"**
- Add the positions with salary estimates
- See the total cost impact immediately
- Compare against budget constraints

**"What's the best way to flatten our management structure?"**
- Create Scenario A: Combine two teams
- Create Scenario B: Add a layer
- Create Scenario C: Remove a layer
- Compare side-by-side to choose the best option

**"How should we handle a 10% budget cut?"**
- Model closing specific positions
- See the org structure impact
- Calculate exact savings
- Identify the least disruptive approach

## The Scenario Lifecycle

Here's how scenarios typically flow:

### 1. Create
- Start from Main Org (current state)
- Choose scope (full org, partial org, or blank canvas)
- Name your scenario (e.g., "Engineering Q2 2026 Reorg")

### 2. Model
- Make changes to positions
- Add new hires
- Close positions
- Move teams
- Edit attributes

### 3. Analyze
- Review the Change Tracker (cost and headcount impact)
- Compare before/after
- Check for unintended consequences

> **[Screenshot placeholder: Change Tracker showing cost and headcount impact summary]**

### 4. Compare (Optional)
- Create alternative scenarios
- Compare different approaches side-by-side
- Choose the best option

### 5. Collaborate
- Share with stakeholders
- Add comments and discussions
- Gather feedback

### 6. Approve
- Submit for approval workflow
- Route to decision makers
- Track approval status

### 7. Implement
- Once approved, work with HR/IT to implement changes in your HRIS
- Scenario remains as a record of the planned changes

## Types of Scenarios

### Full Org Scenario

**What it is:** Complete copy of everyone in your access scope.

**When to use:**
- Company-wide reorganizations
- Annual planning that affects multiple departments
- Strategic planning across the entire org

**Pros:**
- See the full organizational context
- Make changes anywhere in the org
- Understand cross-department impacts

**Cons:**
- Slower performance with large orgs (5000+ people)
- Can be overwhelming to navigate
- Changes may be scattered across many areas

**Example:** "2026 Annual Org Redesign"

---

### Partial Org Scenario (Most Common)

**What it is:** Copy of a specific subset of your organization (one department, division, or team).

**When to use:**
- Department-specific reorganizations
- Team restructuring
- Focused planning for one area
- When you want faster performance

**Pros:**
- Faster to create and work with
- Focused scope keeps you on track
- Easier to share with specific stakeholders
- Better performance

**Cons:**
- Don't see positions outside the scope
- Can't move positions outside the subset
- May miss cross-department dependencies

**How to scope:**
- Select a specific manager and their entire org
- Filter to a department and include reporting chain
- Choose a division or business unit

**Example:** "Commercial NA Q1 2026 Expansion"

**Pro tip:** Partial Org scenarios are the most commonly used. They're faster, more focused, and easier to manage than Full Org scenarios.

---

### New Org Scenario (Zero-Based)

**What it is:** Blank canvas where you build an org structure from scratch.

**When to use:**
- Creating a new department
- Building a greenfield organization
- Zero-based budgeting exercises
- Starting fresh without existing structure constraints

**Pros:**
- Complete freedom to design from scratch
- No legacy structure to constrain thinking
- Forces intentional design decisions

**Cons:**
- Time-consuming (must add every position manually)
- Easy to forget positions or roles
- No baseline to compare against

**Example:** "New APAC Office Build-Out"

## Key Scenario Features

### Change Tracker

**What it shows:**
- All positions added (+cost)
- All positions closed (-cost)
- All positions moved (neutral cost)
- Net headcount change
- Net budget impact
- Total scenario cost

**Why it's valuable:** Instant visibility into the financial impact of your changes. No spreadsheet calculations needed.

---

### Before & After Comparison

**What it shows:**
- Side-by-side visual comparison
- What changed vs what stayed the same
- Reporting relationship shifts

**Why it's valuable:** Quickly verify your changes had the intended effect and catch unintended consequences.

---

### Time-Based Planning

**What it does:**
- Schedule changes for future effective dates
- Model phased hiring (5 positions in Q1, 3 in Q2, etc.)
- Plan reorganizations with specific implementation dates

**Why it's valuable:** Model timing of changes, not just what changes. See when costs hit.

---

### Approval Workflows

**What it does:**
- Submit scenarios to stakeholders for review
- Route through approval chains
- Track approval status
- Comment and collaborate

**Why it's valuable:** Formalize decision-making process. Get sign-off before implementation.

---

### Scenario Comparisons

**What it does:**
- View multiple scenarios side-by-side
- Compare cost and headcount differences
- Visualize structural differences

**Why it's valuable:** Evaluate multiple options to choose the best approach. Present alternatives to leadership.

---

## Common Use Cases

### Annual Planning
Create scenarios for next year's org structure and headcount budget. Model hiring plans by quarter.

### Reorganizations
Test different reporting structures before announcing changes. Identify the cleanest approach.

### Budget Cuts
Model the impact of closing specific positions. Find the least disruptive way to meet budget targets.

### Expansions
Plan new team structures for growth initiatives. Model hiring sequences and reporting relationships.

### Mergers & Acquisitions
Model combined org structures. Test different integration approaches.

### Succession Planning
Model leadership transitions. Test backup plans for key roles.

## Scenarios vs Main Org

| Main Org | Scenarios |
|----------|-----------|
| Current state | Future state / what-if |
| View-only | Fully editable |
| One version | Unlimited versions |
| Synced from HRIS | Manually created/edited |
| Real-time data | Point-in-time snapshot |
| Cannot make changes | Add, edit, move, close positions |

---

## Scenarios vs Forecast

**Scenarios** = Specific org structure planning
- Who reports to whom
- Exact position details
- Reporting relationships
- Visual org chart

**Forecast** = Aggregate headcount planning
- How many hires per quarter
- Budget by department over time
- Multi-year projections
- No specific positions

**Use both together:** Create a Forecast for annual headcount targets (e.g., "hire 50 people next year"), then create Scenarios to model the specific org structures that achieve those targets.

## Best Practices

1. **Name scenarios clearly** - Include date, department, and purpose (e.g., "Engineering Q2 2026 Expansion")
2. **Start with Partial Org** - Faster and more focused than Full Org for most use cases
3. **Create multiple versions** - Make Scenario A, B, C to compare approaches
4. **Use Change Tracker frequently** - Keep an eye on cost and headcount impact
5. **Share early and often** - Collaborate with stakeholders before finalizing
6. **Document your reasoning** - Use comments to explain why you made certain decisions
7. **Don't over-complicate** - Start simple, add complexity only if needed

---

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ **Creating Full Org scenarios when Partial Org would work** - Slower performance, harder to navigate

❌ **Making too many changes at once** - Hard to track what changed and why

❌ **Not checking Change Tracker** - Missing the cost impact of changes

❌ **Forgetting to name scenarios** - Ends up with "Scenario 1", "Scenario 2", "Scenario 3" - confusing later

❌ **Not comparing alternatives** - Choosing the first idea instead of evaluating options

❌ **Skipping stakeholder review** - Surprises during implementation

## Getting Started

Ready to create your first scenario?

1. **Go to Main Org** - Start from current state
2. **Click "Create Scenario"** - Button in top right or from homepage
3. **Choose Partial Org** - Select the department you want to model
4. **Name it clearly** - Include date and purpose
5. **Start making changes** - Add, edit, move, or close positions
6. **Review Change Tracker** - See the impact of your changes
